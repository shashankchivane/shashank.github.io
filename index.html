PM Learnings

Ten years of building at national scale, managing governments, regulators, and engineering teams — across digital infrastructure, AI deployments, and enterprise platforms. These are the lessons that actually shaped how I work, not the textbook version.

🏛️Working with Government & Regulators
Lesson 01
Jurisdiction is the real risk register
On Agristack, the most complex issues weren't technical — they were jurisdictional. NIC owned infrastructure, the Ministry owned policy, and state governments owned farmer data. Mapping who owns what decision before writing a single line of code saved months of rework. Your RAID log needs a jurisdiction column.
DPI · Stakeholder Management
Lesson 02
Policy mandates are fixed; timelines are negotiable
Working under RBI regulation on the Account Aggregator framework taught me that the compliance requirement never moves — but how and when you sequence delivery to meet it usually can. Identifying the non-negotiable constraint early lets you protect it while creating flexibility everywhere else.
Regulatory · Schedule Management
Lesson 03
Build allies before you need them
In multi-ministry programmes, stakeholder conflict arrives without notice. The relationships I had built through routine alignment calls — not crisis escalations — were the ones that unblocked delivery. Invest in relationships when there's no agenda, and you'll have credit when there is one.
Government Delivery · Influence
⚙️Delivery Mechanics & Agile in Practice
Lesson 04
Sprint predictability is a lagging indicator
When I took over the Account Aggregator delivery, sprint completion was at 65%. The problem wasn't team velocity — it was scope leaking into sprints mid-cycle from integration dependencies we hadn't surfaced. Fixing the dependency mapping improved predictability to 88% without changing team size or working hours.
Agile · Metrics
Lesson 05
RAID logs rot if no one owns them
Every project has a RAID log. Most have a RAID log that was last updated six weeks ago. I learned to assign a specific action owner and a hard review date to every item — not just a category. A risk without an owner is just a worry written down.
Risk Management · Process
Lesson 06
Estimation accuracy improves with decomposition depth
The most consistent source of overruns I saw at Media.net wasn't bad estimation — it was estimating at the wrong level of granularity. Tasks estimated at story level were 3× more accurate than tasks estimated at epic level. Getting teams to decompose before estimating is the PM's job, not the BA's.
PMO · Estimation
🤖Delivering AI & Emerging Tech
Lesson 07
AI projects fail at integration, not at the model
Deploying LLM-based tools at Protean, the hardest part was never the AI output quality — it was connecting model responses to legacy system inputs and outputs with no clean API contract. Treating the integration layer as a first-class deliverable (not an afterthought) is what made AI Suites actually ship.
AI/ML · Systems Integration
Lesson 08
Set SLA expectations before the first demo
Stakeholders who see an AI demo expect production performance immediately. On triage automation, setting explicit SLA thresholds before any demo — and explaining the training data dependency — meant we had a shared definition of "working" that wasn't just "as good as the demo."
AI Deployment · Expectation Management
Lesson 09
Human fallback is not optional
Every AI-automated workflow we deployed had a manual escalation path built in from day one — not bolted on after the first incident. Government stakeholders, in particular, need to see that no process is 100% delegated to a model. Designing for human-in-the-loop from the start made approvals faster, not slower.
AI Governance · Risk
👥Teams, Culture & Leadership
Lesson 10
Context is more motivating than targets
Managing 25+ person teams across DPI programmes, I noticed that engineers who understood why Agristack mattered — 40M farmers, consent-based credit access — worked differently from those who just knew their sprint tickets. Connecting the work to the outcome isn't a morale exercise, it's a delivery strategy.
Leadership · Team Dynamics
Lesson 11
Mentoring junior PMs compounds over time
At Iksula, investing time in the junior PMs on my team meant that by month six, I was reviewing their RAID logs rather than writing them myself. The leverage from developing people is higher than any process optimisation. A PM who only manages up has a ceiling; one who develops the team below has none.
Coaching · Delegation
Lesson 12
The PM sets the communication default
Whatever frequency and format the PM defaults to for status updates becomes the team's communication culture. At Media.net, switching from ad-hoc Slack messages to a structured weekly async update reduced "what's the status?" interruptions by more than half. The template matters less than the consistency.
Communication · PMO Culture
☁️Cloud, Infrastructure & Technical PMs
Lesson 13
Cost optimisation needs a PM, not just an architect
The 40% AWS cost reduction on Rajasthan Patrika didn't happen because the architect suddenly had a good idea. It happened because I scheduled a dedicated cost review sprint, created visibility into the billing breakdown, and made cost a tracked delivery metric alongside performance. Engineers optimise what PMs measure.
Cloud · Cost Management
Lesson 14
Load testing is a project milestone, not a QA task
On the Rajasthan Patrika migration, performance at 12,000 concurrent users only held because load testing was a gated milestone — not something left to QA the week before go-live. Technical PMs need to own the definition of done for non-functional requirements, or they quietly disappear from scope.
Performance · QA · Delivery
